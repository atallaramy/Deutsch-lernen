# Research behind the card design

What the evidence says and how each finding shapes the decks. Sources were
checked on 2026-09-25 unless marked; full links are in [links.md](links.md#research-behind-the-card-design).

| Finding | Source | Consequence for the decks |
|---|---|---|
| Recall beats recognition, especially for knowing how a word is spelled. | Nakata 2016, *IRAL* 54(3); the Anki FAQ on multiple choice | Every card is typed recall. No multiple choice. |
| Spaced retrieval with feedback improves long-term L2 learning. | Kim & Webb 2022, *Language Learning* (meta-analysis, 48 experiments); Karpicke & Roediger 2008 (not rechecked, standard reference) | Use Anki scheduling (FSRS, retention 0.90). The back always gives the canonical form first. |
| A single glossed context sentence adds little to learning meaning compared with word pairs. Context helps knowledge of use. | Webb 2007, *Language Teaching Research* 11(1) | A context must teach something (collocation, case frame, register, situation). Otherwise use a bare, precise cue. |
| Similar words learned together interfere with each other. | Tinkham 1993/1997; Nation 2000, *TESOL Journal*; Wozniak rule 11 | Disambiguating cues (*Mama/Mutter*, *Familienname/Nachname*). Interfering cards are kept at least 12 positions apart in the new-card queue. |
| Learning nouns inside larger units with their article helps gender. | Arnon & Ramscar 2012, *Cognition* 122(3) | The Articles back always shows the full chunk (*die Tasche, die Taschen*). Sentence contexts are used when a verbatim nominative slot exists. |
| Explicit, reliable gender cues help novices. | Presson, MacWhinney & Tokowicz 2014, *Applied Psycholinguistics* 35(4); Köpcke & Zubin (principles of German gender assignment) | Pattern hints only when dependable: ‑in for female persons, ‑ung, ‑heit/‑keit, ‑chen, compounds take the last part's gender, natural gender. Exceptions are flagged (*der Name*, *der Buchstabe*, *der Kaffee*). |
| Minimum information; context cues simplify wording; redundancy is fine when the retrieval differs; give sources. | Wozniak, *Twenty rules* (4, 11, 16, 17, 18) | One target per card, situational cues, a source on every card. Two cards on the same word only when they test different skills. |
| Practise in the form you will use it (transfer-appropriate processing). | Morris, Bransford & Franks 1977 (not rechecked, standard reference) | Sentences uses replies to real German lines and du/Sie switches, not translation drills. |
| One new element per sentence (practitioner guidance from sentence-mining learners, not a controlled study). | Experienced-learner practice ("i+1") | The known-word check flags context words you have not captured yet. |
| Anki specifics: typed comparison is exact and single-line; sibling burying works only within one note. | Anki manual | Answers follow German spelling and punctuation strictly. Spacing across decks comes from content and card order, not from Anki. |

The previous deck reference also cited Karatas et al. on spacing, retrieval
and feedback in L2 vocabulary (doi:10.1177/13621688211053525). It was not
rechecked in this session.

## Marking your own writing (extra tasks)

Checked on 2026-10-06; links in [links.md](links.md#research-behind-marking-your-writing).

| Finding | Source | Consequence for the marking |
|---|---|---|
| Correcting a text you were just shown how to fix is not a reliable measure of progress; progress shows in new writing later. | Aliakbari et al. 2023, cited in Nguyen & Chu 2024 (review, *rEFLections* 31(2)) | No automatic second round. Redoing a task later shows the progress, and its comment compares it with the earlier try. |
| Direct correction (the right form given) had a lasting effect four weeks later and needed less mental effort than codes alone. | López et al. 2018, cited in Nguyen & Chu 2024 | Every mistake is shown as You wrote · Correct · Why. |
| Teachers usually give written feedback so that learners can revise their errors. | Ellis 2008, cited in Nguyen & Chu 2024 | A second round stays available when you ask for it. |
| Words need about 15–20 meaningful, spaced exposures; recycle them across lessons and units, not only within one. | Conti 2025 (practitioner summary of vocabulary research); *Repetitions and Recycling in EFL and ESL Teaching* (myenglishpages) | Repetition comes from later lessons and the Anki decks rather than from re-marking the same text. |

Bitchener & Ferris, *Written Corrective Feedback in Second Language Acquisition
and Writing* (Routledge 2012), is the standard book on the topic; its page could
not be opened, so it is not quoted here.
