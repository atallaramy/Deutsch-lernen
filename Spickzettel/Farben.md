# Farben

One colour, one meaning, in my grammar book and all my cheatsheets.

| Colour | Example | Means |
|---|---|---|
| <nom>blue</nom> | ein<nom>e</nom> Küche · <nom>er</nom> ist dunkel | **Nominativ**: the subject, after sein · wer? was? |
| <akk>red</akk> | Hast du ein<akk>en</akk> Stift? | **Akkusativ**: the object, after haben, es gibt · wen? was? |
| <dat>green</dat> | aus d<dat>er</dat> Schweiz · <dat>im</dat> Süden | **Dativ** (DW A1 E9 L3): after aus, in (wo?) · wem? wo? |
| <gen>purple</gen> | das Auto mein<gen>es</gen> Vaters | **Genitiv** (later): possession, "of" · wessen? |
| <verb>orange</verb> | du wohn<verb>st</verb> · Ich <verb>wohne</verb> in Berlin. | **Verb**: in a table its ending, in a sentence the conjugated verb; in the Perfekt both parts |
| *grey italics* | *The garden is big.* | English and notes |
| **bold** | **Der** Garten ist groß. | the word the example is about |

## The rules

1. One colour, one meaning, everywhere and forever. A new colour comes only with my yes, and is added to this list.
2. Gender never gets a colour: m · f · n · pl are columns.
3. What changes with the case takes the case colour: the ending of articles and possessives, later of adjectives (ein<akk>en</akk>, d<akk>en</akk>, mein<nom>e</nom>; no ending, no colour: mein, ein, kein); a pronoun or a short form as a whole word (<nom>er</nom>, <dat>im</dat>, <dat>am</dat>).
4. Colour marks only what changes, never decoration, and only on pages and sheets about cases or verbs. In an example only the word it is about is coloured. Elsewhere words stay black.
5. A second thing in the same table, like e → i in *n**i**mmst*, is bold, never a second colour.
