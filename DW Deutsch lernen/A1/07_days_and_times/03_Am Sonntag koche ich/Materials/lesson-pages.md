## Grammar: Prepositions of time: an

<https://learngerman.dw.com/en/prepositions-of-time-an/l-37452061/gr-38305032>

*Wann sieht Emma fern? –  **Am** Freitag.*

<span lang="DE-DE">When</span> you say on which day you do something, you use the preposition *<span lang="DE-DE">an</span>*<span lang="DE-DE"> together</span> with the article *<span lang="DE-DE">dem</span>*<span lang="DE-DE">. The </span>two words merge to form *<span lang="DE-DE">am</span>*<span lang="DE-DE">.</span> 

<span lang="DE-DE">When</span> *<span lang="DE-DE">am</span>*<span lang="DE-DE"> is</span> used to express time, it is often placed in first position in the sentence or immediately after the subject and the conjugated verb.

## Culture: Leisure activities

<https://learngerman.dw.com/en/leisure-activities/l-37452061/rs-39371120>

Many Germans plan their free time carefully. This is also true for children. Parents often want to give their children the best opportunities from a young age. So don't be surprised if you meet German children who have a whole lot of activities planned after school. Sports are especially popular, like soccer, volleyball, tennis and horseback riding. Lots of children and teenagers also learn a musical instrument.

Some children have a lot of hobbies and a full week, but most would have activities on one or two days a week. It's important to many families that their children have a chance to play during their free time.
