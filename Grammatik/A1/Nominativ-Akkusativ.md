# Nominativ und Akkusativ: subject, object, der/den, ein/einen, kein/keinen

*der Nominativ*, *der Akkusativ* · A1 · from DW A1 E5 L3 *Ich habe kein …*, E5 L4 *Das Auto ist rot* and E6 L1 *So wohne ich* · checked 2026-10-11

## The rule

German terms:
- *das Subjekt* (subject: The subject is the person or thing that is active or central in the sentence. The subject is also known as "der Satzgegenstand". It is usually a noun or pronoun.)
- *das Objekt* (object: The object refers to a person or thing that is the aim of an action or event. The object is also known as the complement. It is usually a noun or pronoun.)
- *der Nominativ* (nominative: The subject is always in the nominative. The articles are: der/ein, die/eine, das/ein, die/-.)
- *der Akkusativ* (accusative: Most objects are in the accusative. The articles are then: den/einen, die/eine, das/ein, die/-.)
- *der Kasus* / *der Fall* (case: Depending on what function the noun has, its form can change. However, this change can best be seen in the form of the article. In German, there are four different distinguishing forms or categories (cases) of noun, known as *Fälle* or *Kasus*.)

1. The **subject** does the action, or the sentence is about it. It answers **Wer?**
   (people) or **Was?** (things). It is in the **nominative**: *Die Frau isst eine Pizza.*
2. Many verbs also need an **object**. It answers **Wen?** (people) or **Was?** (things).
   Most objects are in the **accusative**: *Ich schreibe einen Text.*
3. In the accusative **only the masculine singular changes**: *der → den, ein → einen,
   kein → keinen*. Feminine, neuter and plural stay the same.
4. *sein* always takes the nominative: *Das ist kein Stift.* *haben* takes the
   accusative: *Ich habe keinen Stift.* So does *es gibt*: *Es gibt hier wirklich
   keinen Fahrradladen.* ([Wiktionary: geben](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/geben#German), *es gibt* [with accusative])
5. **kein** is the negative of *ein*, with the same endings. A plural, or a noun with no
   article, also takes *kein*: *Das sind keine Stifte.* · *Nina isst keinen Schinken.*
6. Some verbs with an accusative object: *essen, trinken, nehmen, suchen, kennen, machen,
   lieben, anrufen, besuchen, besichtigen*.
7. German has four cases. You see them best on the article. More come later (the
   dative in DW A1 E9 L3).
8. Which article? The **definite** article (*das*) when you have information about a
   particular person or thing; the **indefinite** article (*ein*) when you have no
   specific information; the **negative** article (*kein*) to say the noun in the negative.

## Table

| | Nominativ (subject; after *sein*) | Akkusativ (object; after *haben*) |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | der · ein · kein Stift | **den** · **einen** · **keinen** Stift |
| feminine | die · eine · keine Brille | die · eine · keine Brille |
| neuter | das · ein · kein Buch | das · ein · kein Buch |
| plural | die · – · keine Stifte | die · – · keine Stifte |

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why | From |
|---|---|---|---|
| einen Brillenetui | ein Brillenetui | *das Brillenetui* is neuter: the accusative stays *ein*. | [my mistakes](<../../DW Deutsch lernen/A1/05_things/03_Ich habe kein/Materials/mistakes.md>) |
| Akkusative | Akkusativ | The German word has no *-e*. | [my mistakes](<../../DW Deutsch lernen/A1/05_things/03_Ich habe kein/Materials/mistakes.md>) |
| Ich habe ein Stift. | Ich habe einen Stift. | *der Stift* is the object of *haben*: *einen*. | rule |
| Das ist keinen Stift. | Das ist kein Stift. | *sein* always takes the nominative. | rule |
