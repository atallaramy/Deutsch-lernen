# du or Sie: informal and formal

*informell / formell* · A1 · from DW A1 E0 L1 *Hallo!*, E0 L3 *Tschüss!*, E0 L4 *Von A bis Z*, E1 L1 *Ich heiße Emma*, E1 L3 *Woher kommst du?*, E2 L2 *Wichtige Nummern*, E2 L3 *Adressen*, E2 L4 *Auf dem Amt*, E3 L1 *Was machst du hier?*, E3 L2 *Was trinkst du?*, E3 L4 *Zahlen bitte!* and VHS A1 Lektion 2 · checked 2026-10-03

## The rule

German terms:
- *informell* (informal: Informal language is used when talking to family and friends.)
- *formell* (formal: Formal language is used in formal situations.)
- *die Höflichkeitsform* (polite form: The formal personal pronoun in the second-person (both singular and plural) is "Sie".)
- *das Personalpronomen* (personal pronoun: Personal pronouns refer to people or things. The words *ich*, *du* and *Sie* are examples of personal pronouns.)
- *duzen* (to use "du" with each other)

1. **du** is informal: family, friends, acquaintances and often colleagues. You use
   their first name: *Hallo, Sarah!*
2. **Sie** is formal: adults you don't know, business, officials, shopping. You use
   *Herr* or *Frau* + surname: *Guten Tag, Frau Kamp!*
3. Not sure? Use **Sie**.
4. *Sie* is the same for one person and for several people. It always has a capital
   **S**, and so do *Ihnen* and *Ihr/Ihre*.
5. The whole sentence follows the choice: the verb (*kommst* ↔ *kommen*),
   *dir* ↔ *Ihnen*, *dein* ↔ *Ihr*.
6. **At an office** (police, registration office, bank) the whole conversation is formal:
   *Guten Tag* (or *Guten Morgen*, *Guten Abend*), *Herr/Frau* + surname, *Sie*,
   *Ihr/Ihre* instead of *dein/deine*, and *Auf Wiedersehen* at the end (DW E2 L4).
7. **Switching to du.** At work people do not use *du* right away. One person suggests it:
   *Wollen wir uns nicht duzen?* To accept: *Ja, gern*. Young people use *du* at once,
   even when they have just met (DW E3 L1).

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why | From |
|---|---|---|---|
| Und ihnen? | Und Ihnen? | Formal *Ihnen* always has a capital I. | [my notes: Hallo!](<../../DW Deutsch lernen/A1/01_Intro_zu_A1/01_Hallo/Hallo.md>) |
| Wie geht es dir, Herr Müller? | Wie geht es Ihnen, Herr Müller? | *Herr/Frau* + surname goes with *Sie*. | rule |
| Wie heißen du? | Wie heißt du? | The verb follows the pronoun: du → *heißt*, Sie → *heißen*. | rule |
