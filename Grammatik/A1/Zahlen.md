# Zahlen: numbers 0–1000 and over

*die Zahl* (number) · A1 · from VHS A1 Lektion 2 and DW A1 E2 L1 *Zahlen von 1 bis 100*, E2 L2 *Wichtige Nummern*, E2 L4 *Auf dem Amt* · checked 2026-09-30

## The rule

1. **0–12**: learn them by heart. *elf* and *zwölf* are irregular.
2. **13–19**: the single digit + *zehn*: *dreizehn*. In 16 and 17 the digit loses its
   ending: *sechzehn*, *siebzehn*.
3. **Tens**: digit + *-zig*: *vierzig*. Irregular: *zehn*, *zwanzig*, *dreißig* (with ß),
   *sechzig*, *siebzig*.
4. **21–99**: digit + **und** + ten, written as one word: *dreiundzwanzig*.
   *eins* loses its -s: *einundzwanzig*.
5. **100**: *einhundert*, short form *hundert*.
6. **Hundreds**: digit + *hundert*: *zweihundert*, *neunhundert*.
7. **101–999**: the hundreds first, then the last two digits as usual, all in one word:
   *zweihundertzwölf* (212), *zweihunderteinunddreißig* (231), *einhunderteins* (101).
8. **Thousands**: digit + *tausend*: *zweitausend*. 1000 is *eintausend*, short form *tausend*.
   Then the hundreds, then the rest: *eintausendfünfhundertzweiunddreißig* (1532).
   In figures you can write a point after the thousands: 1.532.
9. **Saying a long number**: say it whole, or digit by digit. The longer the number,
   the more usual it is to say the digits one by one. Phone and bank account numbers
   are said in single digits or in pairs.

In the first example the phone number is said one digit at a time; Lotte's number
(43 87 55 62) is said in pairs. Repeated digits: *zweimal* (twice), *dreimal*
(three times) + *die* + the digit: *dreimal die Sieben* = 777 (DW E2 L2). *Uhr* stays singular in times (DW glossary:
*in Uhrzeiten: nur Singular*).
