# Grammatikbegriffe: grammar words

Every German grammar word from my studied lessons · checked 2026-10-07

Words that mean the same share a row, split by ` / `. Each word has its English and a
short description of what it is, in my lessons' own words where they give one; its page
gives the same description and explains the word with examples from my lessons. The same
words, with a short example each, are on my one-page cheatsheet
`Spickzettel/Grammatikbegriffe.pdf`.

## Word types

| Term | English | Page |
|---|---|---|
| *das Nomen* / *das Substantiv* / *das Hauptwort* | noun: People and things are known as nouns. In German they are sometimes also referred to as *Substantive* or *Hauptwörter*. | [Genus](A1/Genus.md) |
| *das Verb* | verb: Verbs are words that describe activities, occurrences or a situation, e.g. walk, eat, play. German verbs usually end with *-en* in the infinitive, e.g. *kommen, heißen, lernen*. | [Präsens](A1/Praesens.md) |
| *der Artikel* | article: An article is a word that can come before a noun. The words *der*, *die* and *das* are the three definite articles that indicate the grammatical gender of the noun. | [Genus](A1/Genus.md) |
| *der bestimmte Artikel* | definite article: The articles *der*, *die*, *das* are definite articles. | [Genus](A1/Genus.md) |
| *der unbestimmte Artikel* | indefinite article: The articles *ein*, *eine* are indefinite articles. | [Unbestimmter Artikel](A1/Unbestimmter-Artikel.md) |
| *das Pronomen* | pronoun: a word that stands for a noun | [Personalpronomen](A1/Personalpronomen.md) |
| *das Personalpronomen* | personal pronoun: Personal pronouns refer to people or things. The words *ich*, *du* and *Sie* are examples of personal pronouns. | [Personalpronomen](A1/Personalpronomen.md) |
| *das Indefinitpronomen* | indefinite pronoun: Indefinite pronouns refer to people or things that are indeterminate. They are used when one does not know, or does not wish to say exactly who, or how many, are doing something. | [man](A1/man.md) |
| *der Possessivartikel* | possessive article: shows who something belongs to: *mein, dein, Ihr* | [Possessivartikel](A1/Possessivartikel.md) |
| *das Fragewort* | question word: At the beginning of a W-question there is a question word that usually begins with the letter *W*. | [W-Fragen](A1/W-Fragen.md) |
| *die Präposition* | preposition: Prepositions are usually short words that connect words or groups of words together. They describe the relationship between people or things. | [wohnen in](A1/wohnen-in.md) |

## Nouns

| Term | English | Page |
|---|---|---|
| *das Genus* | grammatical gender: Every noun has a grammatical gender. The genders are masculine, feminine and neuter. | [Genus](A1/Genus.md) |
| *maskulin* / *feminin* / *neutral* | masculine / feminine / neuter: Masculine nouns have the article *der*, feminine nouns have the article *die* and neuter nouns have the article *das*. | [Genus](A1/Genus.md) |

## Verbs

| Term | English | Page |
|---|---|---|
| *der Infinitiv* | infinitive: The basic form of the verb is called the infinitive and consists of the verb stem + "-en": "heißen", "kommen", "wohnen". | [Präsens](A1/Praesens.md) |
| *der Verbstamm* | stem: When conjugating verbs, you remove "-en" from the infinitive form and add the appropriate ending. For example: The root of "lernen" is "lern-". | [Präsens](A1/Praesens.md) |
| *die Endung* | ending: In conjugation, the endings of the verbs often change. The ending depends on which person is doing the action. The ending comes after the verb stem. | [Präsens](A1/Praesens.md) |
| *die Konjugation* | conjugation: When a verb is used in a different tense, or with a different person, it changes. It often gets a specific ending. This change in the verb form is known as conjugation. | [Präsens](A1/Praesens.md) |
| *der Vokalwechsel* | vowel change: In some situations, where the verb stem includes an "e", it changes to "i" in the second- and third-person singular ("du" and "er/sie/es"). | [Vokalwechsel](A1/Vokalwechsel.md) |
| *das Präsens* | present tense: The present is a verb tense. It is used when speaking about events, activities or situations that happen in the present or are continuing to happen. | [Präsens](A1/Praesens.md) |
| *das Präteritum* | simple past: The simple past is a verb tense. It is used in written language to describe events, activities or situations that happened in the past. The simple past of certain verbs, e.g. *sein*, is often also used in spoken language. | [Präteritum](A1/Praeteritum.md) |

## Sentences and questions

| Term | English | Page |
|---|---|---|
| *der Aussagesatz* | statement: In a statement, a fact is declared. The statement may give information or describe a situation. | [Aussagesatz](A1/Aussagesatz.md) |
| *die W-Frage* / *die Ergänzungsfrage* | W-question: W-questions are open questions that cannot be answered with "yes" or "no". They are also called *Ergänzungsfragen*. At the beginning of a W-question there is a question word that usually begins with the letter *W*. | [W-Fragen](A1/W-Fragen.md) |
| *die Ja-/Nein-Frage* / *die Satzfrage* | yes/no question: This is a question that can be answered with "yes" or "no". | [Ja-/Nein-Fragen](A1/Ja-Nein-Fragen.md) |
| *das Subjekt* | subject: The subject is the person or thing that is active or central in the sentence. The subject is also known as "der Satzgegenstand". It is usually a noun or pronoun. | [Nominativ und Akkusativ](A1/Nominativ-Akkusativ.md) |
| *das Objekt* | object: The object refers to a person or thing that is the aim of an action or event. The object is also known as the complement. It is usually a noun or pronoun. | [Nominativ und Akkusativ](A1/Nominativ-Akkusativ.md) |

## Cases

| Term | English | Page |
|---|---|---|
| *der Kasus* / *der Fall* | case: Depending on what function the noun has, its form can change. However, this change can best be seen in the form of the article. In German, there are four different distinguishing forms or categories (cases) of noun, known as *Fälle* or *Kasus*. | [Nominativ und Akkusativ](A1/Nominativ-Akkusativ.md) |
| *der Nominativ* | nominative: The subject is always in the nominative. The articles are: der/ein, die/eine, das/ein, die/-. | [Nominativ und Akkusativ](A1/Nominativ-Akkusativ.md) |
| *der Akkusativ* | accusative: Most objects are in the accusative. The articles are then: den/einen, die/eine, das/ein, die/-. | [Nominativ und Akkusativ](A1/Nominativ-Akkusativ.md) |
